MOON PHASES

The Moon is always half illuminated by the Sun. Its phase is the portion of that sunlit half visible from Earth. A complete phase cycle takes about 29.5 days.
🌑 New MoonNear 0% visible illumination
🌒 Waxing CrescentIlluminated portion is growing
🌓 First QuarterAbout half illuminated
🌔 Waxing GibbousMore than half and growing
🌕 Full MoonNearly the whole visible disk illuminated
🌖 Waning GibbousThis image · more than half and shrinking
🌗 Last QuarterAbout half and shrinking
🌘 Waning CrescentThin crescent before New Moon
Waxing = illumination is growing · Waning = shrinking · Gibbous = more than half illuminated.

OBSERVATION INFO

↔ Best viewed in landscape mode on mobile.

Photographed in Singapore on 1 September 2026 from 01:50–02:15 SGT as a high-resolution 83-panel lunar mosaic. Each overlapping panel was built from a stack of 100 selected video frames, giving 8,300 source frames across the complete Moon.

Many short-exposure frames are stacked to reduce the effects of atmospheric turbulence and improve fine detail. The processed panels were then assembled into one continuous mosaic. For this interactive globe, the finished BQAstro photograph was georeferenced to lunar latitude and longitude so the feature markers align with their real positions.

MOON FACTS

Mean radius: 1,737.4 km
Diameter: 3,474.8 km
Surface gravity: 1.62 m/s²
Average Earth distance: ~384,400 km
Sidereal orbital period: 27.32 days
Synodic phase cycle: 29.53 days
Escape velocity: 2.38 km/s
No substantial atmosphere
The Moon rotates synchronously with its orbit around Earth, so approximately the same hemisphere normally faces Earth. Libration lets us see somewhat more than half of the lunar surface over time.
